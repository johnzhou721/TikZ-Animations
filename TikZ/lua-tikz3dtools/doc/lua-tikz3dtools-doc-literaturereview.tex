\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Existing Approaches in Practice and Academia}

Current methods for 3D occlusion include \(z\)-buffering and the 
painter's algorithm. \(z\)-buffering approximates visibility by 
tracing rays from the viewing plane and assigning each pixel the 
color of the first intersected triangle. The painter's algorithm 
orders primitives from back to front \cite{nyberg2011hidden}. 
Centroid sorting, a variant used for parametric surfaces, orders 
primitives by the average depth of their vertices 
\cite{skillmon2025answer3dpointsort}. Depths can be computed as a 
dot product with the observer vector \cite{5062592} or by aligning 
the \(z\)-axis with the observer vector via an affine transformation.

Camera placement is typically handled by applying an affine 
transformation to move the camera, then performing the inverse 
transformation on all objects. Perspective projection is applied 
afterwards using projective transformations \cite{rogers1976mathematical}. 
While standard in graphics, these techniques do not guarantee exact 
occlusion or primitive intersections, and have not been implemented 
rigorously in \TeX{}-based systems \cite{chernoff2025creating}.

\section{Limitations and Open Challenges in Current Methods}

Existing methods do not compute intersections between primitives 
and cannot deterministically resolve occlusion. \(z\)-buffering is 
approximate and only works at the pixel level. Centroid sorting 
produces errors even for non-intersecting primitives 
\cite{jasper2025tikzparametricsurface}. Attempts to refine it, 
such as grouping by normal orientation, remain insufficient. No 
general algorithm exists that deterministically handles visibility 
for non-intersecting, non-cyclically overlapping primitives, let 
alone one that could be applied in a \TeX{} environment 
\cite{5063772}.

\section{Proposed Approach and Its Advantages Over Existing Work}

\luatikztdtools{} addresses these limitations with a pipeline that 
operates directly on simplices:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Objects start as \(0\)--\(2\)-dimensional affine simplices 
  \cite{jasper_texse}.
  \item These simplices are transformed by affine or perspective 
  projective matrices \cite{rogers1976mathematical}.
  \item Transformed simplices are intersected to produce the correct 
  subdivision of space \cite{5063772}.
  \item An occlusion comparator deterministically orders the resulting 
  simplices by visibility \cite{jasper2025tikzparametricsurface}.
\end{itemize}
This approach produces exact, mathematically guaranteed occlusion, 
unlike \(z\)-buffering or centroid sorting. It provides both a 
practical rendering tool and a pedagogical framework, exposing the 
geometry and linear algebra underlying 3D visualization. The 
pipeline is rigorous, transparent, and fully reproducible, making 
it a contribution that would be significant even outside \TeX{}.
