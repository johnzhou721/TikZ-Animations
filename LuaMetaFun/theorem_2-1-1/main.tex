def render_theorem_box =
numeric w, h;

    \startbuffer[BufA]
        \startframed[myframed]
            {\bf Theorem 2.2.1} Congruent arcs (of the same circle) are 
            subtended by congruent chords.\color[white]{ That is, if two arcs of a 
            circle are congruent, then the chords that subtend them are 
            congruent as well.\par
            {\it Proof.} Suppose there is a circle, and two arcs in 
            this circle are congruent: \im{\cup AmB=\cup CnD}.
            According to the definition of congruence, we can use a 
            rigid motion (isometry) to superimpose \im{\cup CnD} on 
            \im{\cup AmB} so that these two arcs will totally coincide.
            Suppose (for certainty) that point \im{C} coincides with 
            point \im{A}, and \im{D} coincides with \im{B}.\par
            Then, by AXIOM 1, there is one and only one straight 
            segment that connects points \im{A} and \im{B}. Thus,
            as a result of this isometry, segment \im{CD} will 
            coincide with \im{AB}, in other words, the two segments
            are congruent. {\bf Q.E.D.}}
        \stopframed
    \stopbuffer
